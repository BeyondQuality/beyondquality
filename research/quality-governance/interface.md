# The governance-management interface for quality

Part of [Governance of quality](quality-governance.md).

This page describes what flows between the top and the departments below it.

The interface is everything that passes between the top and the departments below it: what flows down from governance, what flows up from product, engineering and QA and from every department that answers for a number the top has published, and who decides what along the way. Most companies already have the reporting lines. What is missing is the content: nothing flows up in comparable units, nothing flows down as stated appetite. (The same mechanism would serve any risk-bearing function, security included; this research develops it for software quality and product work, see the [scope](quality-governance.md).)

## What flows down

In the standards' vocabulary, five things come down that only the top can ratify:
- the purpose, which states what the organization is trying to achieve;
- the value objectives, which state what quality is worth pursuing and why;
- the quality targets, which state how well the product must satisfy which needs and segments;
- the risk appetite, which states how much exposure is acceptable in pursuit of the objectives;
- the delegation limits, which state who may decide what, and up to which size of decision.

Most of these exist in companies already, only unstated: budget decisions imply an appetite, and every deadline fight implies what a delay is worth, even though nobody ever wrote either down. Through the interface, the top passes all of them down inside one explicit, published object, the six-part statement that [value.md](value.md) constructs; what a particular delay or failure costs is worked out from it case by case (the decision rule below). Written down together, the statement is what the standards call a governance policy: the governing body's intentions and direction, formally expressed (ISO 37000 3.2.9), with the risk appetite among its contents (38500 7.2.4).

The statement's six parts carry those five governance parameters alongside the baseline numbers, minimums and default rule needed to operate them:
- **Part 1, the opening:** the *purpose*, where the company intends to win, which practices are governed (including building software with AI agents when used; [erosion.md](erosion.md)), and the two-step decision rule;
- **Part 2, the numbers and their owners:** the baseline numbers each department tracks and answers for;
- **Part 3, the bets:** one line per customer or internal need the company invests in, stating the chosen level (*quality target*), tolerated shortfall, expected return range (*value objective*), holding cost range and review date;
- **Part 4, the required minimums:** obligations set by law or contract, with the safety margin and assurance spend around each;
- **Part 5, the common rule:** keeping every other need as good as usual, normal engineering care included, with no extra gold-plating;
- **Part 6, the risk appetite and delegation limits:** numerical exposure bounds per kind of risk (including knowledge loss under AI), the size of decision above which management escalates, the conditions on the people who oversee tools or agents, what tools or agents may not decide alone, and what requires an explicit sign-off from above.

[value.md](value.md) describes each part. The framework says what must be readable and leaves the choice of tool to the company. A statement counts as published only when it is used: teams decide against its numbers, the reports refer to them, and the decisions, written acceptances and sign-offs are kept. Otherwise the policy exists on paper only (38500 7.2.7).

The governing body publishes the statement and answers for it (38500 6.1). The whole statement is remade and published anew at each regular reevaluation, when the top re-asks the two questions; between reevaluations, each bet has its own review date, and the top reopens an entry at once when an event changes its stakes.

## The decision rule

In daily work, the statement's bounds and delegation limits become a two-step decision rule. For any case such as "ship now or test more", product with engineering estimates what waiting to test or fix more on this particular feature would cost the company, the feature's delay plus the extra checking or fixing work (there is no company-wide price for a day of delay, because features differ in what their delay loses), and QA with engineering estimates what shipping now risks, the expected failure loss anchored on the bet's money worth in the statement and how likely the failure is. First, before looking at delay cost, the team checks whether this decision exceeds its escalation limit or whether adding this risk would push total exposure or performance past the published red lines: Part 4's required minimums, Part 3's shortfall floors and Part 6's numerical risk appetite for that kind of risk. An expensive deadline never permits crossing a red line: if the risk crosses any of them, the team cannot approve shipping on its own, and either reduces the risk first or escalates the case with both numbers attached. Second, when the risk fits inside all the red lines, the team weighs what waiting costs against the expected failure loss that waiting to test or fix would remove, and takes whichever option leaves the company better off, alone, with no meeting and no escalation. The argument is no longer won by authority or by pushing harder (what the [problem statement](quality-governance.md) promises under Decision). The first check is the standard's boundary, acting within the authority governance established while accountability stays above (38500 3.7, 6.1; ISO 37000 4.2.2); the second check inside that boundary comes from [Reinertsen's flow economics](quality-governance.md#references).

## What flows up

The top has published the six-part statement. What comes back are the reports through which the top learns whether its numbers hold, and the record from which it answers for what the company tolerates.

Every report refers to a number the statement itself published. A bet is settled in the numbers of the department that backed it; a risk report is compared against the published appetite for that kind of risk. The [problem statement](quality-governance.md) says that today nothing flows up in comparable units; under the statement every report is measured against its published bound in the same unit, and pricing the bets and exposures in money alongside departmental units lets the top compare exposures across functions. The top receives summaries; test results, review notes and the rest stay below, with management. The economics research describes how every risk stays tied to its evidence ([step 3](../testing_economics/step3.md)), so when the top wants to check a number, the detail behind it can be pulled up on demand.

At each reevaluation, the top also has someone check the reporting itself, not just single numbers: someone who does not write the reports takes a few of them, traces each back to the raw material behind it, and sees that the numbers match reality and that the machinery works, that breaches really get reported at once and that risk acceptances really get recorded in writing. A false report looks exactly like a true one, so the check runs on that schedule, when nothing looks wrong, and never only on suspicion. ISO 37000 asks the body to assure itself that the reports and evidence it receives are accurate (6.4.3.1 d); 38500 asks for assurance over the governance framework and oversight controls (5.6.3), feeds its results into the regular assessment of the framework (7.2.1), and points at auditing as a further source of it (7.2.7).

Five kinds of reports travel up.

1. **Each bet, checked at its review date.** The department that backed the bet puts what actually happened next to the range it expected (deals or conversions counted for a bet on winning, quality-attributed loss and renewal evidence alongside whether the level held for a bet on protecting). Whoever did the work of holding the level puts the actual cost next to the estimate. Both halves of every bet get checked: what it earned or protected, and what it cost. Its owner adds one line of context, and when a number missed its range says why, as best they can tell; answering for a responsibility includes reporting and explaining how it was carried out (ISO 37000 3.2.2, Note 1). There are three usual answers when a bet missed: we estimated wrong, we did not do the work we planned, or the world changed. Each answer calls for a different correction, so without the why, the top cannot know what to fix.

2. **The current size of each risk.** For every kind of risk listed in the statement, management reports how big that risk is currently estimated to be, next to the published appetite for that kind. Where agents write or test the code, QA marks any risk whose checks lack a human source or a source independent of the code under test (because tests generated from the code confirm its own faults) as unsupported ([implementations.md](implementations.md), [erosion.md](erosion.md)). The economics research supplies the machinery for measuring this on the quality side ([step 2](../testing_economics/step2.md) and [step 4](../testing_economics/step4.md)). The top sees one picture, in the numbers it itself published.

3. **What management accepted in writing.** The risks management chose to accept within its authority, each signed by its owner with the reason and a review date; [step 2](../testing_economics/step2.md) of the economics research describes the acceptance decision. These records are what the top answers from when someone asks what the company tolerates and why.

4. **Breaches, and the warnings before them.** A level that fell short by more than its bet tolerates; a legal or contractual minimum broken or nearly broken; a risk that outgrew the published appetite for its kind; a failure that reached customers, with what it cost. Each report says which published number it broke. Breaches serve two purposes at once: they show where the estimates were wrong, and they belong on the record the top answers from. The warning comes before the breach: when a risk keeps growing and is getting close to the agreed limit, management says so while there is still time to act; when the number behind a bet keeps sliding toward the edge of what is tolerated, its owner flags it before it falls out (38500 7.2.6). Ten small breaches count as one big problem: when small breaches recur in the same place, management reports the pattern right away even though no single one was large on its own (5.5.2). When the broken level is one the company promised outside, in a contract or an SLA, reporting it inside is not enough: the affected customers must be told promptly ([value.md](value.md), Part 3).

5. **Proposals to amend the statement.** When a department's worsening number or a newly identified risk points at a need without a bet, when a bet's stakes have disappeared, when the money set aside in a bet cannot hold the chosen level, or when a risk bound or delegation limit (such as how much work may be handed to an agent in one piece) needs adjusting, management proposes the change. The top decides, at review dates, at once when a limit cannot hold, or whenever the whole statement is remade ([value.md](value.md), the needs without bets and keeping the model; [implementations.md](implementations.md)).

Each report has its moment. A breach, or a warning that one is coming, is reported at once; that is what the appetite and the minimums are for. The current risk sizes and the risks management accepted in writing are reported on a regular schedule that management keeps; for parts of the system that change quickly, the reports come more often, because a quarterly report is useless for something that changes every week (38500 7.2.6). Each bet is checked at its own review date, set in the statement. When something big changes, a new law, a market shift, a new kind of deal, the top gets fresh reports on the affected entries right away, the same way it reopens the affected bets, instead of everyone waiting for the next schedule (6.2.2). At each reevaluation, when the top re-asks the two questions, the bet checks, the current risk sizes, the breaches and the proposals are gathered in one place alongside every department's refreshed Part 2 trend numbers, including those covering needs without bets ([value.md](value.md), step 2 and the needs without bets).

Each department reports its own actual numbers, the same numbers it answered for in the statement. Management measures the risk sizes, records what it accepted, and assembles the reporting. The top holds the record it answers from. Nobody reports anyone else's numbers. Without the upward flow, the top just issues orders and never learns whether they work. Governance is both flows together.

## The loop

Run continuously, the interface is a loop. The top sets the parameters in the statement, from the whole picture. Management turns them into work: it pursues the quality targets, runs the prioritized risk register, and decides the everyday cases inside the published parameters. The four-step loop of [Economics of Testing](../testing_economics/testing_economics.md) is that machinery on the quality side; [implementations.md](implementations.md) describes it step by step, as it runs today and as it changes when the governing body takes part.

The reports flow back up and go to two places:
- The bet checks, the proposals, the refreshed Part 2 trends, the current risk sizes and the breaches go into the reevaluation, where the top corrects the statement's numbers (otherwise they stay last year's guesses), reviews its appetite and changes the set of bets. This half returns to [value.md](value.md), where the regular re-asking lives.
- The risk sizes, the written risk acceptances, the explicit sign-offs and the breaches also stay with the top as the record it answers from, to owners, auditors, regulators, or a court if it comes to that.

Risk sizes and breaches go to both places: they inform the reevaluation, and they go on the record. The statement gets refreshed, and the loop runs again; it never ends by design. In economic terms, the company keeps each level where its worth supports it, no higher and no lower, and every exposure within its appetite. That is one half of the "maintain" promised in the research's [purpose statement](quality-governance.md): the loop keeps the numbers current. The other half, keeping the loop itself alive, is [the erosion thesis](erosion.md)'s subject.
